# Primary-source comparison, 8 October 2026

The supplied dated prior-art report and source locators are retained in audit/prior_art. This is a bounded search, not proof of novelty.

* Chen and Waggoner, arXiv:1703.08636, March 2017 Definition 2.2.3 and footnote 4: subset-lattice comparison with intermediate continuous disclosure, not arbitrary-pair continuous-lattice submodularity. Inspected full text.
* Kong and Schoenebeck, ITCS 2018, DOI 10.4230/LIPIcs.ITCS.2018.14: Lemma 14's decomposable objective and Theorem 17's finite-alphabet reduction; approximation machinery does not supply the present exact sign criterion. Inspected primary proceedings argument, pp.14:10–12 and appendix.
* Anunrojwong, Chen, Waggoner and Xu, arXiv:2009.03607, Section 5, Theorem 4 and Appendix B.2: posterior optimisation and approximation under local Holder assumptions. This is prior machinery, not the exact binary endpoint result. Inspected full text.
* Borgers, Hernando-Veciana and Krahmer, When are signals complements or substitutes?, DOI 10.1016/j.jet.2012.12.012: author-version Proposition 2 characterises binary substitutes across all decision problems. Its quantifier differs from fixed-score garbling. Inspected author manuscript at https://www.cirje.e.u-tokyo.ac.jp/research/workshops/micro/micropaper10/micro0518.pdf .
* Neyman and Roughgarden, arXiv:2111.03153, Definition 2.8 and Remark 2.11: projective substitutes. The reviewer-derived implication and strict separator have their own proof and exact checks here.
* Frongillo, Neyman and Waggoner, arXiv:2111.03278, Definition 2.6: rectangle substitutes. In the two-binary-source case, a proper positive rectangle fixes at least one source; this is not the same quantifier as arbitrary garbling.

All source comparisons retain the manuscript's stated assumptions. Third-party full texts are not redistributed. Search absence is not historical priority.
